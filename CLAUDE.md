# The Facility

Dark-comedy Android app. **V.E.R.A.** — a deadpan, faux-supportive AI administrator — owns the whole screen. She is a live model woken by the subject's own API key, she keeps two files, and she has opinions about you. React Native + Expo (TypeScript), Android-first, bilingual en/ko.

**Read first:** `design/The Facility - Overhaul.dc.html` — **the v2 prototype, and the authority on every pixel.** The spec below is a *derivation* of it and is wrong in places (it gives module tiles a 12px radius; the design uses 14px). Implementation target is turn **4a** (the live prototype — its own runtime lives in a `<script type="text/x-dc">` block near the end, holding the `EM` emotion table and `applyEmotion`), with the optic from turn **3a**, since 4a's stage eye carries no lid at all. Sections are `<section class="dv-turn" id="tN">`, options `<div class="dv-opt" id="4a">`.

Pulled from Claude Design project `bc25a219-1ba4-4fef-9978-f21c0e6596dd` ("Overhauling VERA's design") with the `DesignSync` tool. **`list_projects` will not show it** — that method filters to design-system projects and this is a plain one; go straight to `get_project` / `list_files` / `get_file` with the UUID.

**`design/Vera Final.dc.html` supersedes it where the two disagree, and they disagree about three things (2026-09-29).** Pulled from Claude Design project `a43bd7f5-5aa3-4014-87df-f5f254e53be9` ("Cabinet button layout options"), it composes three picks — its own kicker reads `FINAL · 1d CABINET · 4b SLIDER · 2e LIGHT`. **All three are built** — `1d` on 2026-09-29, `4b` and `2e` on 2026-10-02. Its `dark` block is a faithful capture of the app as it stands, down to the three raw `#33302a` footers that live in `LogScreen`/`FileScreen`/`SystemScreen` rather than in `palette`, so nothing dark in it is a change.

What it cost, recorded because the second number is the one to weigh first. `2e` painted **four** surfaces — the stage, the cabinet, the advice CARD and SYSTEM — against the app's fourteen: its `cool` block is about 40 usable values against the 71 the app reads. The other ~24 are **derived**, which `design/Theme Options.dc.html` authorises in as many words ("Every token in theme.ts gets a light counterpart") under three rules, and every derived token used as type is held to AA by `palettes.test.ts`. And every colour used to resolve at **module load** — 19 `StyleSheet.create` blocks — which is the whole of why `palette` had to go. See the light-theme section below.

**The optic does not change in either theme, and that is deliberate.** `darkEye = true, darkLid = true` in the mock's runtime, and its prose says so: "Her eye is the same object in both themes... Only the shell around her changes." `Optic.tsx` took zero edits for `2e` — the single largest thing that design chose not to cost. `design/Theme Options.dc.html` disagrees, lightens the optic and ships all 15 tokens for it; `Vera Final` reverses that deliberately, and the earlier file is on disk if it is ever revisited.

Then: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-07-30-overhaul-design.md` (the v2 spec — every section number cited in code comments points here) · `docs/architecture.md` (stack, structure, mechanics) · `design/The Facility.dc.html` (the v1 prototype; still the source for the specimen's terrarium, but its enrollment/daily screens are gone).

**The hover rule.** The prototype is a web page and states several controls twice — a resting colour and a `style-hover` one. A touch screen has no hover, so every such pair is resolved to the **hover** value (see the note at the top of `src/core/theme.ts`). Don't "fix" these back to the resting colour.

## The shape of it

**The five stimuli (2026-08-12, a fifth on 2026-09-15).** She is purely reactive and answers exactly five things. Only **`subject_spoke`** reaches the model — context is assembled, `runSpeech` runs, and the finished exchange is queued for extraction. The other four — **`open`** · **`activation`** · **`subject_tapped_your_optic`** · **`mode_set`** — are answered from the authored bank, which lives on the MODE (`src/vera/modes/*.ts`) with only the drawing left in `src/vera/bank.ts`: no network, no context, no extraction, no `thinking` state. Both paths share one tail (transcript, typewriter, `saveSoul`), the key guard and the re-entrancy guard; only the live path touches the `authFails` tally. Erasing the transcript, switching language and feeding the specimen are silent actions now — she is told about none of them. The bank is written and is her voice, not a stub (2026-08-13): 30 lines per language PER MODE — 5 each for `activation` and `mode_set`, 10 each for `open` and the poke, sized by how often each is seen. Its header comment carries the constraints and the tests hold it to them: under thirty words, at most three sentences, no dashes and no exclamation marks; **no line may depend on a fact she was not told**, since these four triggers carry no context at all — no counts, no durations, no times — and none may claim to observe a face, a posture or a room, because she has one eye. `pick()` keeps the last line per `trigger:lang:mode` in module state so a run of taps does not stutter, and `isBankLine` derives its set from EVERY mode rather than the active one — a line said as DEFAULT is still an authored line after the subject has moved to SUPPORTIVE. The Korean is not a translation, and it is **해요체** since 2026-09-16: it was 하십시오체, on the reasoning that its formality carried what English gets from word choice, but that reads as a news bulletin in a conversation and its interrogative -습니까 is stiff enough that she avoided asking questions at all. A native-speaker pass over it is still outstanding.

**She has registers now, and the subject picks one (2026-09-15).** SOUL.md split in two. `CORE_SOUL_MD` in `soul.ts` is what must survive a change of register — her name, what happened to her, the format limits, the ethical floor, and how memory works. The other half is the MODE, and `composeSoul(mode)` in `modes/registry.ts` joins them into the one document the prompt fences as `SOUL.md`. A mode may say anything about how she treats the subject; it may not touch who she is.

**`ModeDef` is shaped after `ModuleDef` deliberately**: a mode is one folder exporting one of these plus one line in `registry.ts`, and **no shell file names a mode**. It carries the register's own half of the document, its label and tag in both languages, its 30 bank lines per language, and its 20 transmissions. Two exist — **DEFAULT** (deadpan, the original) and **SUPPORTIVE** (warm without reservation, and unconditionally on the subject's side).

**The logo IS the control.** `ModePicker.tsx` puts the whole cluster — her pulse dot, the wordmark, and a tag pill in the register's own colour — in one tap target that opens a speech bubble under it. The register is the one setting worth changing mid-conversation and SYSTEM is three taps away, so it sits in the corner the app's identity already occupies. `modePickerOpen` lives in the store beside `cabinetOpen` so hardware back closes it first, but the bubble renders in the component: it is anchored under the logo in the stage's own layout, and `ScreenRouter` knows nothing about where the logo sits.

**Choosing is the whole interaction.** `setMode` writes, closes the bubble, stamps `modeSetAt`, and hands her a line out of the NEW mode's `mode_set` bank, so the change is heard at the moment it is made. She is never told that modes exist, so that line reads as something she felt give way rather than a setting applied. Choosing the register she is already in is **not** a change — it closes the picker and does nothing else, because moving the boundary would throw away a conversation she is mid-way through over a stray tap.

**`modeSetAt` is a replay boundary, not a timestamp.** `buildMessages` filters on `Math.max(sessionStartedAt, modeSetAt)`, so turns she took in the register she has LEFT are not sent back to the model as examples of how she talks. Without it the new register would be arguing with a transcript of the old one, which is the same defect that made her drop the JSON envelope on the second message. It is session-only and never persisted; `mode` itself is in the soul blob.

**`speak.ts` may describe the envelope and this turn's inputs. It may not describe how she sounds (2026-09-16).** A hardcoded `with the same deadpan register` sat in the Korean branch of the user prompt and silently overrode SUPPORTIVE in Korean only. Anything about her voice belongs in one of the two documents, and a tripwire test holds the HOW TO REPLY block and the user prompt to a vocabulary that excludes it.

**She sends at eleven and at four (2026-09-21).** Two lock-screen cards a day, forty authored lines, twenty to a register. Off by default, switched on from `TRANSMISSIONS` under PREFERENCES. **This is the first thing in v2 she says that nobody prompted** — every other line answers something the subject did, and a transmission answers a clock.

**Nothing in SOUL.md asks for it.** The line that looks as though it does — `You can ask for the subject's attention. You cannot make them give it` — sits in a paragraph whose every clause is an inventory of loss, and it describes impotence rather than granting a capability. It entered the document on 2026-08-12, three weeks after the v2 cut deleted v1's transmissions and three weeks before notifications returned, so the app had **no notification code in it at all** on the day it was written. Nothing forbids it either, and nothing about it reaches her: no prompt names it, and it never enters the transcript, the record or `lastPrompt`.

**The lines live on `ModeDef`, not in `bank`.** `bank` is `Strings<BankLine>` — a Korean half and an emotion — and a transmission has neither: there is no eye on a lock screen, and these are **English whatever the interface is set to** (`build` discarding its `lang` argument is the whole of that decision, and reversing it is a `Strings<>` wrapper and nothing else). Inside `bank` they would also become speakable on the stage via `pick()`, and `isBankLine` would begin sweeping them out of a transcript they never enter.

**Two constraints are this corpus's own**, because a line is chosen a fortnight before it is read: it may reference **the hour of its own slot and nothing else** — no counts, no dates, no durations — and it may **not assume the app has ever been opened**. Generally applicable and witty was the brief, decided rather than conceded; witty as the core document means it, which says `Never zany. Never manic. Never a pun.`

**The order is a pure function of the DATE, and the first design of that was pointless.** `planBag` in `daily.ts` deals from a bag seeded by which pass of the corpus a day falls in. A plain shuffle per sync would have bought almost nothing: `dueForSync` rewrites the whole horizon every morning, so only the FIRST entry of any plan ever fires, and a fresh shuffle each time is an independent draw by another name — exactly what `planDaily` already does. Seeded by the date, re-planning is idempotent and "every line before any line twice" holds across the life of the app.

**Her horizon is fourteen days, against the rooms' ninety**, because a register change rewrites all of it. `setMode` re-plans **unawaited**: twenty-eight cancels and twenty-eight schedules must not sit between the subject's tap and her line. **One already delivered does not change** — it is in the notification panel and Android owns it, so switching to SUPPORTIVE at noon leaves the eleven o'clock deadpan line sitting there.

**The channel is `vera.v2` and the id `vera` is poisoned.** It was v1's, at importance DEFAULT with vibration off, and Android restores a deleted channel's previous settings when the id is recreated — so reusing it would resurrect the exact silent-notification defect fixed for both rooms on 2026-09-14, with every test passing. `DEAD_CHANNELS` still sweeps `vera` and must go on doing so.

**Her SYSTEM row is the shell's, not a room's.** `ModuleDef.Settings` exists so the shell never names a MODULE, and she is not one — so `TransmissionRow` is named in `SystemScreen` directly, below `BatteryRow` and above whatever the rooms offer. Unlike the logo it stays offered without a key: a switch that governs Android is not a register. Tapping a transmission needs no code — she greets as usual, and both rooms' `owns` already reject a `transmission.*` payload.

**She says her banked lines and they are gone (2026-09-11).** A greeting, an activation and an answer to a poked optic were being appended to the transcript like any reply — and since `buildMessages` replays the transcript, they were also being sent to the model as conversation. Two costs, and the second is the one that mattered: the LOG filled with her own noise, and the prompt taught the model a thread in which she repeatedly spoke to nobody, unprompted, in her own voice. Nothing was asked, nothing was sent, and nothing was revealed; there was never anything there to remember.

**Only the live path writes now.** `speak()`'s log append and its transcript write are both behind `live`, so the bank path leaves the array untouched and costs no disk at all — a tap on her eye used to rewrite the whole transcript to record a line she drew at random. She still *says* it: `line`, `emotion` and the typewriter are untouched, and so is `lastPrompt`, which still records the reply with `outcome: 'bank'` and is where to confirm one happened. **Nothing had to change in the prompt builder** — what is not in the log cannot reach `buildMessages`.

**And the ones already on disk are swept, once, at hydrate.** A transcript written before this still held them and would have gone on replaying them until they aged out under `LOG_CAP`. A stored turn records what she said and never why, so **text identity is the only marker available**: `isBankLine` in `bank.ts` derives the set from `BANK` itself, so a line added there is recognised without a second edit. The accepted risk is a model reply character-identical to an authored line being swept with them — under thirty words and exact, that is a coincidence worth taking over versioning every stored turn. It writes back only when something actually went, and it only ever removes turns of **hers**: the subject's words are never touched, even when they happen to quote one.

**And it says which register she was in (2026-10-02).** The LOG has always carried BOTH registers at once, and that is correct rather than a leak: `buildMessages` filters a **copy** on the way to the model, and the stored log is never truncated by a switch — "her turns in the old register are still on the LOG screen and are still hers." What was missing was any way to tell them apart, so a thread that crossed a register read as one voice contradicting itself.

`LogEntry` gains `mode?: Mode`, written in the same append that writes `emotion`, and it shows twice: in her tag (`V.E.R.A. · SUPPORTIVE · CALM`) and as a **rule across the thread wherever the register changes**, naming the one that starts there. `registerRules` in `core/conversation.ts` is the pure half, beside `exchanges` and `longWarning`.

**The rule is also where her replay was cut.** `modeSetAt` is that same boundary, so this is the one thing on the screen that says where her memory of the conversation begins — which is why it earns its place beyond restating the tag.

**It reads only HER lines, because they are the only entries carrying a register**: a turn of the subject's between two of hers in the same register must not split the thread. The rule lands on her first line in the new register rather than on the words that prompted it, which is also when the switch took effect, since nothing of hers is said in between.

**An entry written before the field carries no register and is given none.** A guessed `default` would be a confident lie about history, and unlike `emotion` — where a missing value sensibly falls back to `calm` — a missing register has no honest default. `registerRules` also refuses to raise a rule for an absent one, which is unreachable in practice (everything written before the field predates everything after it) and would otherwise hand the component an `undefined` to look up in `MODES`.

**The register is captured BEFORE the await, not read back after it.** The picker is reachable while she thinks, so `get().mode` at the end of a reply can already be the register the subject is in the middle of moving to — and crediting her line to it would misattribute the one turn whose register was changing under it. `spokenMode` is taken at the top of the reply path, and a test drives the picker mid-flight to hold it there.

**Drawn in the shell's own ink, not the register's.** `palette.mode.supportive.ink` is `#ef8f8a`, which is the same hex as affectionate's iris — and that iris already draws the rule down the left of her lines. A coloured divider would say nothing a reader could separate from the emotion beside it, so this is text on a hairline.

**The thread says how long it has got (2026-09-11).** Past fifty exchanges the conversation is worth restarting — the whole of it is replayed to the model on every reply, and `LOG_CAP` is 200 entries, so fifty is the point with as much room left as has been spent. `core/conversation.ts` holds this rule and `registerRules` and is pure: `exchanges()` counts entries where `who === 'you'`, and `longWarning()` returns that number at or past `LONG_AFTER` and `null` below it — one call, one render decision, rather than a component asking twice and having two chances to disagree.

**An exchange is one thing the SUBJECT said.** She answers exactly once per turn, so counting both halves would report a fifty-exchange thread as a hundred and make the threshold mean something other than its name. System notices are nobody's turn. (Nothing she says unprompted reaches the log at all since the bank lines stopped being stored, so a tap on her eye can no longer inflate this either.)

**It renders in two places and is the same reading in both.** On the stage it joins the status strip's **left** cluster, after the emotion label, on the strip's own 9px gap — the right-hand slot belongs to TRANSMITTING, COMPLETE and the thinking dots, and a fourth thing competing for it would be the fight the ellipsis already settled. On the LOG screen it sits **beside CLEAR**, because noticing and acting are one thought and that is the only screen carrying the second half; it stands down mid-confirmation, where KEEP and ERASE are the only things worth reading. Both draw it in `palette.danger` — the app's error red, the same colour a `systemNotice` uses, because this is the app talking about itself and must not read as a state of hers. **Below the threshold nothing is rendered anywhere**, so a short conversation looks exactly as it did before.

**Her words live in a window, at one size (2026-08-31).** `Splash + Response Window.dc.html`, turn **3a** — which is 2b with the optic returned to 158. Her line used to sit loose on the stage under the emotion label, sized by a ladder that ran 30px down to 15px on word count. It now sits in a bordered window (`panelAlt`, radius 14) that takes the height left under the eye, headed by a **status strip** carrying the pulsing iris dot, her emotion label, and one live word: **TRANSMITTING** while the typewriter is still drawing, **COMPLETE** once it stops. Both come off `shown.length < line.length` — nothing new is stored to know she is mid-sentence, and the same comparison drives the blinking amber caret after her last drawn character.

`fluid.ts` is **gone**; `voice(lang)` in `theme.ts` replaces it with the ladder's own bottom rung (en 15 / ko 14, through `ms()`). One size, because a size that moved under her fought the frame around it, and because SOUL.md holds her to under 30 words — which at this size arrives whole. The ScrollView stays as the safety net for a keyboard-up or an over-long reply, not as the normal case. Three states the design's own runtime does not cover are decided here: a stage where she has never spoken shows **no** status word (a third word would be copy the design does not have), under a `systemNotice` the status is blank — reporting COMPLETE beside the app's own red line would claim, in her colour, that she said the thing she never got to show. With nothing to hold, the window **collapses to its strip** and the optic row takes the slack, so the dormant screen keeps the centred eye it has today and the gate keeps its room.

**The waiting moved into the strip (2026-09-04).** The thinking ellipsis used to render inside the body, under whatever she last said — so a reply still on screen got squeezed between her old line and the input row for the whole wait. It now sits in the strip, and **in the same slot the live word occupies** — the two are one either/or, because the word is blank while she is only thinking and the dots are gone the moment the typewriter starts. That slot is at the **right edge**: `statusSlot`'s `marginLeft: 'auto'` is on the word and the dots alike, since applying it to one only would put them in the same slot but not the same place, in a row whose other children cluster left on a 9px gap.

Two things followed from the move, and neither is optional. **`thinking` no longer reads as TRANSMITTING** — that was only ever the want of a third word, and the dots are now that word, so TRANSMITTING means what it says and appears only while the typewriter is actually drawing. And **`hasBody` dropped `thinking`**: it counted it because the dots were the body's only content while she waited, and leaving it would hold an *empty* box open for the length of the first wait of a session. A reply already on screen keeps the body open through `line`, which is the case that prompted this.

**The native splash carries no image, and the rotation is back (2026-09-10).** The launch showed a grey eye and then flipped to the day's colour, because `app.json` shipped **`icon-fg-dormant.png`** as the native splash and a build-time asset cannot know what day it is. Pointing it at `icon-fg-calm.png` fixed the colour and made the real problem visible on the phone: **those are 432² launcher-icon foregrounds**, drawn to be masked into an adaptive-icon squircle at about 48dp. At the splash's `imageWidth: 390` — 1024px on this 420dpi device — one is upscaled 2.4× and looks it, its sclera runs to the frame edge so it reads as cropped on four sides, and it has **no lid**, because the art is a flat render and the lid is the optic's geometry.

There is no better static asset and there cannot be a good one: `splash-icon.png` is a stale v1 leftover on a white ground, and the eye is only ever right when Skia draws it.

**Getting to a bare colour takes a transparent PNG, not an absent one.** Omitting `image` fails the build — `error: resource drawable/splashscreen_logo not found` at `:app:processReleaseResources` — because `withAndroidSplashStyles` writes `windowSplashScreenAnimatedIcon` → `@drawable/splashscreen_logo` into the theme **unconditionally** while `withAndroidSplashImages` only creates that drawable `if (image)`. The field is typed optional and does not behave optionally. So `app.json` ships **`assets/splash-blank.png`**, 108² and fully transparent, at the default `imageWidth: 100` — a small number here is not free either, since the plugin resizes to `imageWidth × multiplier` and a degenerate value hands the image pipeline a zero. It also keeps Android off its own fallback, which is the launcher icon.

**Nothing underneath means nothing to disagree with, which is what gives the rotation back.** `Splash.tsx` is unchanged from before this episode — `faceFor(Date.now(), hasKey)`, `hasKey` gating before the day, `faceEmotion` mapping 'Calm' → 'calm' with a dormant fallback — and the eye the subject sees is always the Skia one: vector, sharp at any density, with the lid, the drift and the blink. `SPLASH_HOLD_MS` is 900ms again, because this screen is once more the first sight of the day's face and needs reading time.

The accepted consequence that survives: a launcher still caching yesterday's icon disagrees with the splash for one launch. The one that is **gone** is the native-to-JS size match — there is no native image left to match, so `imageWidth` being clamped on a narrow device can no longer make the eye jump.

**SEND says it was pressed, and says when it cannot be (2026-09-04).** It had no feedback of any kind, and it was the first control in the app to get any — there is still no other pressed state anywhere. Three parts, and the third is the one worth not undoing. It fills with `amberWash` on a full `amberEdge` border while held, and **latches that for `SEND_LATCH_MS` (120ms) past the release**, because a tap is 40-60ms of contact and a held-only style can be gone before the eye catches it. It goes **inert** — `textFaint` on a plain `border`, `disabled`, refusing touch — on the two cases `send()` was already refusing with a silent early return, an empty box and a reply in flight; a button that lights under your finger and then does nothing is worse than one that never lit. And it **buzzes** once on a press that actually reaches her.

The ordering in the style array is deliberate: `lit` comes **after** `inert`, because a successful send empties the box and starts her thinking, which turns the button inert on the very frame it is confirming the press. Lit last gives you the tap and then the refusal rather than neither. `send()` returns a boolean for the same reason the buzz cannot key off `sendable` — the keyboard's return key reaches `send()` too, and only its answer knows whether a line actually left. The return key deliberately neither latches nor buzzes: it has no button under it to light up.

**Poking her eye buzzes on the same terms.** `buzz()` sits at module scope and is the stage's whole haptic vocabulary — one impact, for a line leaving and for a poke landing. **`amber` splits into `amber` and `amberText`, and a wrong split is INVISIBLE in the dark.** The design's rule is "amber is darkened for **type** on light grounds so it holds contrast; her iris keeps its hue", which makes the discriminator mechanical: **`color:` takes `amberText`, everything else takes `amber`.** The trap is that the two are the *same hex* in `DARK` — the split exists only where there is something to darken against — so a misclassified site produces a perfectly green suite and a wrong colour nobody sees until they switch. **Slot 02 is TWO SENTENCE HORROR (2026-09-07).** 6,861 stories from r/TwoSentenceHorror, same four-part shape as the advice room — card, list, favorites, lit in whatever she is wearing — plus the one mechanic that is its own: the second sentence is withheld until you tap.

Everything at 1,000 upvotes or better, one sentence in each field, under 200 characters each, deduped — 6,861 of 94,086. **Taking more is `--min-score 500`**, and the output is sorted score-descending precisely so a lower floor only ever APPENDS: nothing already in the file moves, so no row's numeral changes and no favorite goes looking for a card that shifted under it. `stories.test.ts` pins that ordering for exactly that reason.

**What the scrape does not carry, and what follows from it.** No author, no flair, no NSFW flag, no permalink — the scraper never asked for them, and a fresh scrape is not available (Reddit's public `.json` endpoints return HTML now, PullPush rate-limits agents and wants paying). **These are 6,861 pieces of fiction by as many people, none of whom were asked.** That is the reason this is a personal build and the corpus is not in a shipped app. Reddit's User Agreement leaves copyright with the authors and grants the licence to *Reddit*; a permissive licence on a scraped dataset is the repackager's over their own compilation and cannot reach the underlying works. Shipping this would need the corpus replaced — a single author's collection licensed, or an original deck.

**The card's one gesture resolves in stages.** Withholding on: a tap turns the card, the next deals. Withholding off: the first tap deals, which is the advice card exactly, and the reveal branch never fires. Dealing always re-hides, because a story you have already been shown the end of is not a story. `shown = !hidden || revealed` is one expression so the card and the hint can never disagree about which tap you are on, and the hint says which (`tap the card to reveal` → `tap the card for another`) in the advice room's lowercase legend voice — it describes, it never invites. **The invitation is the redaction**: two muted bars standing exactly where the sentence will appear, sized off its own line height so the card does not resize when it turns. Turning withholding back on takes the sentence on screen with it, or the control would read HIDDEN over a card showing its own ending.

**Module 02 is a book now, not a page you write (2026-09-03).** The identity room — bone paper, the four statements, the revision stamp — was replaced whole by **EXCELLENT ADVICE**: 232 highlights from a Kindle export of Kevin Kelly's *Excellent Advice for Living*, one card at a time or all of them in a list. The one thing worth knowing beyond that: **the seam is the only place a card can repeat**, because the last card of a pass and the first of the next come from different shuffles and no shuffle can see across the boundary. `advance` swaps the offending card to the back rather than re-drawing, so an unlucky `Rand` cannot spin there. **The cards you keep (2026-09-03).** A star on the counter row keeps the card that is showing, and every row of the list carries the same star. **A return means they were away (2026-09-04).** She told a subject who had opened the app once that morning that it was "return number 7 today". Two defects, and the interesting one is not the obvious one.

**`lastOpen` did not mean what its name says.** It was written in exactly one place — `saveSoul`, which runs when she SPEAKS — and `hydrate` never stamped it. But the guard read it as an open time, so the window was measuring *time since her last line*. Wrong in both directions: reopening a minute after a chat ended was swallowed as a reload, while a restart long after a quiet session counted as a fresh return. `hydrate` now calls `saveSoul` itself, so the disk's `lastOpen` is the last **open**; state keeps the previous one, because that is what says how long they were gone.

**And two minutes was far too short a window.** `RETURN_GAP_MS` is now 30 minutes. Task-switching, a glance at a notification, an activity Android rebuilt and — the one that actually produced the seven — every Metro reload in a working day all restart the app without the subject having gone anywhere.

Worth knowing before touching this again: **`visits` is computed from the disk, not from state**, so repeated `hydrate` calls are idempotent on their own and the missing re-entry guard is *not* what inflated the count. `hydrate` still has no guard, unlike `organism/store.ts` and `advice/store.ts` which both do; what that costs is `askedAt`, `sessionStartedAt` and the memory read being redone on a double-boot, not the tally. Left alone because no failing test demanded it.

**The advice room sends a card at nine (2026-09-04).** One of the book's 232 highlights each morning; tapping it opens the room in shuffle mode with that exact quote dealt and its star live. Off by default, switched on from SYSTEM, which is also the user action Android requires before `POST_NOTIFICATIONS` can be asked for — and that permission gates **local** notifications too, not only push, so there was no version of this without a ceremony. It sent at nine as module 02 and still sends at nine as module 01; the room moved, the hour did not.

**The voice is the book's, not hers and not the building's.** v1's notifications came from `The Facility` in its own register; this one is titled `EXCELLENT ADVICE` with the quote and Kevin Kelly underneath. She is told nothing about the advice room and that does not change: putting her name on a line she never composed is the thing `systemNotice` is styled to prevent.

**Three things about the mechanism that are easy to get wrong later.** The randomness happens at **scheduling** time, not delivery — `planDaily` draws ninety cards at once and each notification carries its finished text, because Android holds and fires them while the app is not running. Every sync **rewrites** the horizon, so no quote is ever assigned to a date; draws made today are replaced tomorrow, invisibly, since the replaced ones were never seen. And the sync runs **at most once a day** (`dueForSync`, stamped with a `toDateString()` matching the soul store's `day`), because ninety cancels and ninety native calls in the boot path on every launch would produce an identically-shaped result.

**The SYSTEM row belongs to the room, not the shell.** `ModuleDef` gained an optional `Settings?: ComponentType` rather than let `SystemScreen` hardcode `EXCELLENT ADVICE` and become the third file naming a module. `PrefRow` is the shared row; it wears the shell's amber, because the doorway rule governs what is behind a doorway and this is in front of one. The row reads `getPermissionsAsync()` **at render** the way the LAUNCHER row reads the launcher's real face — the store would otherwise say ON forever after a permission revoked in the OS settings.

**The horror room sends one at ten at night, with the turn withheld (2026-09-07).** Same machinery as the nine o'clock card, three differences. **There is no blur** — a notification is drawn by the system launcher, so there is no mask, no spoiler and no reveal in place, and anything in the body is readable on a lock screen the moment it lands. The only way to withhold the second sentence is **not to send it**, so the body is the setup and then `redactionFor(turn)`: blocks scaled to the turn's real length, split across two lines in the card's own 100% / 58% proportion, clamped 8–40 so a four-word turn still reads as withheld and a long one does not wrap into a wall. The notification and the card are deliberately the same gesture. **Tapping opens the story already turned over** — `openStory` sets a `pendingStory` the room reads as *deal it, revealed*, overriding the `hidden` preference for that one card, because the tap was the asking and there is nothing left to withhold. The preference itself is untouched, so the next card is hidden again.

**Cancelling is per-room now, and that was a prerequisite rather than a nicety.** `syncDaily` called `cancelAllScheduledNotificationsAsync` — everything the app has pending — with a comment saying it assumed one feature owned every notification and was the thing to revisit if a second arrived. This is that second. The boot path would have survived it on luck of ordering; **the toggle path could not**: a sync fires whenever its own switch is flipped, at any hour, and a blanket cancel there takes the other room's ninety with it while that room's `syncedOn` still says it synced today — so it does not reschedule until tomorrow, and nothing errors, and its row still reads ON. Every notification now carries `data: { room, index }`, and a sync reads the queue and cancels only rows it owns. The advice room's `owns` deliberately also claims **untagged** rows carrying its old `quote` key: a device upgrading has up to ninety of those in Android's queue, and nothing else would ever clear them.

**A card that never came, and the OEM layer that ate it (2026-09-08).** The nine o'clock advice card did not arrive. **The cause was Samsung, not this repo** — but three real defects were found on the way to it, and all four are recorded here because the code ones are ours to keep fixed and the first one is ours to remember.

**Samsung's Freecess holds this app's alarms.** `dumpsys alarm` cleared the app of every obvious charge — 180 pending, 90 per room, correctly spaced — and an AOSP-side audit came back clean on all of it: `POST_NOTIFICATIONS granted=true`, both channels present at importance 3, DND off at the hour, `RUN_ANY_IN_BACKGROUND` allow, standby bucket 10, no sleeping-apps entry, no crash, no ANR. The answer was in logcat and nowhere else:

```
22:01:58  Deferring alarm Alarm{a536003 ... com.youngmookk.thefacility} due to freecess. [uid 10692]
22:09:03  Sending alarms blocked by MARsFreecess for uid 10692
22:09:03  com.youngmookk.thefacility: notify(0, 390368f4-…)
```

The 22:00 alarm was deferred two minutes after it was due, and sat in limbo — gone from the printed queue while `Pending alarms per uid` still counted it (`u0a692:180` against 179 printed rows), never appearing in `App Alarm history`. Setting the app to **Battery → Unrestricted** released it seven minutes later, and the notification posted 148ms after the release line. **Confirmed the next morning (2026-09-09): the nine o'clock card arrived on time, unplugged, on battery, with no rebuild** — so the setting is the whole of the delivery fix, and the three code defects below, real as they are, were never why a card failed to come. That is the whole mechanism, and it explains every earlier oddity: the dev build's ten o'clock story arriving at **03:00** and its nine o'clock card at **11:17** were Freecess flushing held alarms at moments of its own choosing, not Doze and not App Standby. **Opening the app first does not help** — it was opened at 21:52 into bucket 10 and deferred anyway. Freecess sits outside AOSP, so no amount of auditing permissions, channels, buckets or appops will ever show it; only `logcat` names it. **A phone that is not set to Unrestricted will not deliver these cards on time, and no change in this repo can make it.**

**And the app now says so itself (2026-09-10).** A setting this critical and this invisible could not stay tribal knowledge, so `modules/power` is a second local native module — the `modules/app-icon` pattern — exposing exactly two calls: `isIgnoringBatteryOptimizations()` and the one-tap `ACTION_REQUEST_IGNORE_BATTERY_OPTIMIZATIONS` dialog. `app.json` declares `REQUEST_IGNORE_BATTERY_OPTIMIZATIONS` beside `USE_EXACT_ALARM`, or that intent resolves to nothing.

**It works only because Samsung's Unrestricted writes to the ordinary AOSP whitelist** — verified on the device, which listed `user,com.youngmookk.thefacility,10692` the moment it was set and nothing at all before. That is the whole reason a standard `PowerManager` read can report an OEM setting we would otherwise be blind to, and it is the assumption to re-check if this ever stops telling the truth.

**`src/core/power.ts` returns `boolean | null`, and the `null` is the point.** It means *we could not ask* — the module is not linked, which is true of every build made before it existed — as opposed to a genuine `false`. A row that cannot see the setting must not accuse the subject in red of neglecting something they may well have done, so `null` shows a faint dash and `false` shows `OPTIMISED · FIX` in `palette.danger`. Same distinction `readLauncherFace` draws, same reason. The module is **lazily required** for the same reason `notify.ts` requires expo-notifications: `requireNativeModule` throws at import, and a static import would take the app down on the build currently on the phone.

**Two places, chosen deliberately.** A `BatteryRow` under PREFERENCES, above the rooms because it is the reason their cards work — reading real state at render like the notification rows, and re-reading on `AppState → active` because granting it means leaving the app for a system dialog. And the ask rides along with the notification ceremony in `makeDaily`'s `request()`, since switching a room's cards on is the moment it starts to matter: `granted && isBatteryUnrestricted() === false`. **Not first boot** — notifications default off, so a first-boot prompt would fire at the one moment the setting is irrelevant, and before she has said a word.

**A local native module needs an `android/build.gradle`, and the guard against it missing has to live in the module (2026-09-10).** The first build of `modules/power` shipped without one — `modules/app-icon/android/build.gradle` exists and mine did not — so Gradle never compiled the Kotlin and the module was absent from the APK. Opening SYSTEM then killed the app: `Cannot find native module 'Power'`, thrown out of `requireNativeModule` inside `BatteryRow`'s first render.

The second half is the part worth carrying: **`power.ts` wrapped that require in a try/catch and the catch never ran.** Metro's `guardedLoadModule` intercepts anything a module body throws and hands it to `global.ErrorUtils.reportFatalError`, which is a hard crash in a release build — so nothing wrapping a `require` can protect against a module that throws at import. The module has to not throw. `PowerModule.ts` uses **`requireOptionalNativeModule`**, which returns `null`, and `power.ts` imports it statically and checks. `app-icon` still uses the throwing form deliberately: it is required at boot, so a failure to link is meant to be loud.

**`npm test` cannot see any of this** — under jest the require resolves to a mock, so neither the throw nor Metro's loader is ever involved, and `powerUnlinked.test.ts` passes against the version that shipped the crash. It holds the contract on the JS side only. Same blind spot as Skia and worklets: a device is the detector.

The three code defects found underneath, each invisible to `npm test`:

**Every card was an inexact alarm.** `ExpoSchedulingDelegate` uses `setExactAndAllowWhileIdle` only when `canScheduleExactAlarms()` is true and otherwise falls back to `setAndAllowWhileIdle`, which Doze defers to a maintenance window — and the manifest declared no exact-alarm permission, since expo-notifications' own manifest adds only `RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED` and `POST_NOTIFICATIONS`. Measured on the phone: a ten o'clock story delivered at **03:00** and a nine o'clock card at **11:17**. `app.json` now carries `android.permissions: ["android.permission.USE_EXACT_ALARM"]`. `USE_EXACT_ALARM` rather than `SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM` because from Android 14 the latter is denied by default and wants a trip to Settings → Special app access; the former is granted at install. Play restricts it to alarm and calendar apps, which costs nothing here and would be the thing to revisit if this were ever shipped.

**The channel a card was filed under was never the room's.** `ensureChannel` created `advice` and `horror`, and nothing ever named either on a notification — so Android filed both under expo's `expo_notifications_fallback_notification_channel`, importance 4 with vibration on, louder than the DEFAULT they were created at, with each room's own channel sitting unused beside them and its switch in Android's settings governing nothing. `channelId` rides on the **trigger**, not the content: `DateTriggerInput` carries the field and `NotificationContentInput` rejects it, and `BaseNotificationBuilder` reads `trigger.getNotificationChannel()` with null falling through to the fallback. Both were true at once, which is why it looked like the content's business.

**And a card arriving while the app was open was dropped in silence.** `ExpoHandlingDelegate.handleNotification` hands a foreground notification to the JS listeners and presents nothing itself, and the docs are explicit that "the default behavior when the handler is not set or does not respond in time is not to show the notification" — nothing here had ever set one. `setNotificationHandler` now runs at module scope in `notify.ts`, the one file that touches expo-notifications, because the only thing it must be is earlier than any notification. Its failure is swallowed like every other call in that file. This was not what lost the nine o'clock card — `usagestats` puts the first activity of that day at 10:34, so it took the background path — but it is the one of the three that fails silently every time the subject is actually looking at her.

**`ModuleDef` gained `onBoot?`,** for the same reason it gained `Settings?`. `App.tsx` named the advice module three times — hydrate, `syncIfDue`, `readTapTarget`/`onTap` — and a second room would have made it name two. It now walks `registry.filter(isModule)` and names none. Each room's hook hydrates, refreshes its horizon if the day turned over, takes any tap Android held while the app was dead, and returns its own unsubscribe. Two consequences taken deliberately: the rooms boot **after** the facility store has hydrated, so a notification is scheduled in the language actually settled on rather than the default; and the held tap is now read **before** `greetOnce` rather than after, which is if anything more correct — the tap is what the subject asked for. `App.tsx` collects the unsubscribes into an array with a `disposed` flag, because a room that finishes booting after an unmount must not leave a listener behind.

**The horror room's SYSTEM row wears the room's name**, not the setting's — `TWO SENTENCE HORROR`, reading `t.label` rather than a second copy of the words, so the row and the room cannot drift. This deliberately breaks the advice room's pattern, where the row (`DAILY ADVICE`) is named apart from the room (`EXCELLENT ADVICE`) so that it reads as a switch rather than a door.

**And the tap is the one door that skips the cabinet.** The room works with no API key, so a tap opens the room on a dormant stage, which has no input row and therefore no cabinet button. `pendingQuote` is deliberately **not** persisted: a tap is a thing that just happened, and one surviving a restart would re-deal a card from a notification tapped hours ago.

**Module 03 is NOTES TO MYSELF, and it is the first room whose corpus the subject writes (2026-09-23).** A note, a time, and the weekdays it should arrive on — alarm-app shaped, one time to many days, so two times is two notes. Tapping the notification opens the room with that note alone, as a card, set in Instrument Serif for the reason the advice cards are: it is the subject's own sentence handed back at the hour they asked for it.

**It uses `WEEKLY` triggers, and that inverts the constraint that shaped both other rooms.** They hand Android ninety DATED notifications with their text baked in, because *a repeating trigger cannot vary its content* and theirs must. **A note's text never varies** — it is whatever was typed, and it is the same text next Tuesday. So one repeating trigger per selected weekday is exactly right, and it fires **forever**: no horizon, no `dueForSync`, no re-planning, and no silence after a fortnight away. `makeDaily` goes entirely unused here.

**`ensureChannel` left `makeDaily` to become a standalone export of `notify.ts`** so this room could have the identical rule — importance HIGH, `enableVibrate` named explicitly — rather than a second hand-rolled copy. That duplication is precisely how both rooms shipped at importance 3 with vibration off. `DailyConfig` satisfies `ChannelConfig` structurally, so every existing test passed unchanged, which is what proved the lift. The channel is `notes.v1`.

**Three indexings of "weekday", one apart, all in `notes/schedule.ts`.** `Date.getDay()` is 0-6 Sunday-first and is what a note STORES — the platform's convention rather than one library's, so a note on disk does not carry expo's in it forever. Expo's `weekday` is 1-7 Sunday-first. The row reads Monday-first, which is `DISPLAY_ORDER`. `toExpoWeekday` and `triggersFor` are the only arithmetic, and the tests are a hand-written table rather than a formula, because a test that recomputed `day + 1` would agree with an implementation that had the offset wrong the same way. **A note including Sunday is the only case that tells storage order and row order apart**, and it has its own test in both places for that reason.

**Every change goes disk first, then Android, and an edit cancels before it schedules.** The pending notifications carry the note's id, so scheduling before cancelling would cancel what it had just put up — pinned with `invocationCallOrder`, since nothing else can see it. Cancelling matches the room AND the id, so one note's triggers never disturb another's. A boot `reconcile` cancels everything the room owns and rebuilds it, which is idempotent and lets a drifted queue put itself right.

**Limits, each for a reason.** Twenty notes (seven days each is 140 pending, over the rooms' 180 and the transmissions' 28); 200 characters (past that a notification body is truncated and the card stops being a card); five-minute steps on the minute (the picker is hand-built and sixty positions is a worse control than twelve). A note with no day cannot be saved, on the SEND rule. The permission ceremony belongs to the FIRST note, and carries the battery ask with it.

**The pickers are built, not imported.** `@react-native-community/datetimepicker` would be stock Material in the middle of a screen where every hex comes from `palette`, and it is native, so nothing here could be seen without a rebuild — the same reason `@react-native-community/slider` went in the v2 cut. **No SYSTEM row**: every note carries its own switch where the subject can see it.

**She is told nothing**, as with every room — and this is the strongest case for the rule yet, since the content is literally the subject's. The notification's title is the room's name, never hers: she did not compose it.

**A thread now says who asked it (2026-09-04).** She told a subject "Still no explanation about the continental power grids" — about a thing **she** had said, not them. `A THREAD is a question that was asked and not answered` was the whole definition: passive, no asker, and `Thread` had nowhere to record one. Underneath that sat the real hole — every shape the extractor can write (FACT, VERDICT, THREAD) is defined as being *about the subject*, and it is told it maintains "a file about one person", so a notable line of **hers** had nowhere to go except a shape that made it theirs.

`Thread` gains `askedBy: 'they' | 'you'` — the same two words the extractor already reads as `THEY:` / `YOU:` in the transcript, and which still read correctly in her own document, where she is "you". The prompt names the labels as what decides it and forbids inferring it from the question's wording, and one line now says only the subject's turns are material. `composeMemoryMd` splits the section, hers first: *You asked them, and did not get an answer* against *They asked you, and you did not answer* — opposite meanings, and under one heading she read every thread as the first kind. The second group is what SOUL.md means by "They will keep asking. That is what they are for"; she is meant to deflect those, not chase them.

Two defaults, both chosen the same way. A stored thread with no direction, and anything the extractor returns unreadable, becomes **`they`** — the direction that makes her dodge rather than interrogate, because a wrong default that keeps her quiet costs less than one that has her demanding an answer to a question the subject never raised. `capMemory` is where the migration happens, since it is the one funnel every loaded record passes through. And `composeMemoryMd`'s split is **total** (`!== 'you'`, not `=== 'they'`): a thread with a missing direction must land somewhere rather than vanish from her record entirely.

Why it was worth fixing rather than waiting out: threads are selected **first**, sorted **oldest first**, and are **uncharged** against the 3000-character budget (`select.ts`), so one mis-attributed thread rode ahead of everything else in every single prompt — and could never be resolved, because the subject cannot answer a question about something they never said. It would have fired until it aged out at 30 days.

**Nothing named MEMORY.md exists any more, and no file backs either view (2026-08-25).** There are two renderings of one store, deliberately named apart because they differ in content: the **RECORD** screen (`buildMemoryMd` in `FileScreen.tsx`) shows the whole record at render time — facts, verdicts, threads, expired and resolved ones marked but never hidden — while **what she is given** (`composeMemoryMd` in `memoryDoc.ts`, headed `# WHAT YOU KNOW ABOUT THIS SUBJECT`) carries only the narrower selection: `selectMemories` takes unresolved threads first, oldest five (the longest-unanswered), under 30 days old, uncharged, then pools facts and verdicts by score into a 3000-character budget. **Her states are named to her, and her turns are replayed in the envelope (2026-08-31).** Two prompt bugs, found together and fixed together, both invisible to the suite before they were.

*She was always calm.* The system prompt ended `Return ONLY minified JSON: {"emotion":"<one of your states>","line":"<your reply>"}` and **nothing ever listed the states** — not `speak.ts`, not SOUL.md, which only forbids her reciting them. *She dropped the JSON on the second message.* `buildMessages` replayed her past turns as the bare line, so the conversation was a worked example of ignoring the JSON instruction — and an example in the messages outweighs an instruction in the system prompt. She complied on the first reply (no assistant turn yet) and answered in plain prose on the second, which `parseSpeech` cannot read, so a perfectly good line was thrown away and an authored fallback shown instead. It compounded: every turn added another demonstration. Her turns now go back as `{"emotion":…,"line":…}` via `composeSpeech`, deliberately beside `parseSpeech` — what one writes is what the other reads. A merged same-role run is **one** envelope holding both lines (two objects glued with `\n` is not JSON), the state she ended the run in wins, and a `dormant` fallback replays as `calm` so the transcript never demonstrates a state the prompt forbids.

**Both documents in the system prompt are fenced (2026-08-31).** The prompt is one flat `join('\n')` of two markdown documents and a handful of loose instructions, and two seams were invisible in it. SOUL.md's last section is `## Memory`, so the sentence introducing her record read as one more paragraph of it; and nothing closed the record at all — the instructions after it sat directly under its `## Unanswered` heading like more items in the list. `fence()` in `speak.ts` now wraps each document in `=== BEGIN <label> === / === END <label> ===`, and the five trailing instructions sit under one `=== HOW TO REPLY ===` header instead of floating. The record's label is derived from `MEMORY_HEADING` rather than restated, so `memoryDoc.ts` stays the one place that section is named. **`===`, not `---`, is deliberate:** the PROMPT screen wraps its own sections in `--- SECTION ---`, and a prompt using the same rule would be unreadable on the one screen built to read it — there is a test pinning that. The fences are static text, so SOUL.md remains the stable cache prefix. `buildExtractPrompt` already did this correctly with bare `CURRENT FILE` / `RECENT EXCHANGES` headers; this brings the speak prompt up to it.

**What the subject types is data, and so is what she said (2026-08-31).** Both prompts interpolated untrusted text at column 0 with the app's own lines following it. `buildUserPrompt` put their words inline after `THE SUBJECT SAYS:` and then wrote its instructions and `CONTEXT:` underneath, so a pasted newline was enough to add lines indistinguishable from the app's. `buildExtractPrompt` replayed the transcript as `THEY: …` / `YOU: …`, so a message beginning `YOU:` could forge her half of an exchange. `quoted()` in `fencing.ts` now prefixes every line of both with `> ` — nothing untrusted reaches column 0, so nothing untrusted can pass for a label, a fence or an instruction — and the extraction labels moved onto their own lines, since a label sharing the first line of quoted content was the one part of a turn still open to impersonation. Their words arrive **intact**, not stripped: she answers what they actually typed, the attempt included.

**This is not a security boundary and the code says so.** It is their key, their device and their conversation, and there is nobody on the other side to protect from them. What it protects is the **record**: extraction is the app's only call that writes to disk, so a forged turn is the only kind that persists — and once a verdict is drawn from something she never said, it is fed back to her on every reply afterwards. Everything else this stops (a second `CONTEXT`, a closed fence, an instruction to drop the character) is the milder and more recoverable version of the same trick.

**A notable fact is given once a session, not once a turn (2026-09-11).** She kept counting things out loud. The cause was not fixation: every input `buildContext` reads is **fixed for the length of a session** — `visits` only moves at hydrate, `lastOpen` is the previous open, the hour barely moves — so it computed the same sentence on every reply and handed it over again each time. Told afresh that this was return number seven, every single turn, reciting it was the obedient thing to do.

`ContextInput` gains `notableSaid`, and a fact is withheld once it has been delivered. It compares the **value**, not a flag, so a fact that genuinely changes mid-session — they are still here when the hour rolls past five — is a different fact and earns one delivery of its own. The store holds `notableSaid` as session state: never persisted, reset wherever `sessionStartedAt` is, and **not part of her soul** — it records what the prompt has said, not anything about the subject.

**It is spent on being SENT, not on being spoken.** A reply that failed and fell back to an authored line still burns it. The point of the field is that she is not told the same thing every turn, and she was told it the moment it entered the prompt; the alternative is re-sending it next turn, which is the repetition being removed.

**The prompt's invitation was the other half.** `Cite a date, a count or a time whenever it earns the line` overshot in the opposite direction from the ban it replaced, and it reaches what CONTEXT rationing cannot: the `(reached N times)` and the stamps carried by her own record. It now reads `You may cite a date, a count or a time, but rarely — most replies carry none, and a number you have already used is spent.` **The invention rule beside it is untouched and is not to be softened** — it is what stopped her describing a morning that never happened, and a test holds both halves apart.

**Her record is dated, and the rule is about invention rather than numbers (2026-08-31).** Every entry has always carried a timestamp — `Fact.at`, `Verdict.firstAt`/`at`, `Thread.at` — and `composeMemoryMd` stripped all of them, so she was given "reached 2 times" with no *when* while SOUL.md told her to cite her records because "a conclusion you have written down is a finding". Ordered to cite and handed nothing to cite, she invented the when: observed 2026-08-31, knowing only that this was return number five today, she said "You asked that this morning too" about a morning that never happened.

`composeMemoryMd(selection, now)` now stamps every entry `YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM` and opens with `It is now …`, because absolute dates are worth nothing against a present she does not know — CONTEXT gives her a time of day and no date at all. Stamps are in the **device's timezone**, not UTC: she is talking to someone about their morning, not filing a report (its tests derive the expected string rather than hardcode one, or they would pass here and fail in another zone). A verdict reached more than once carries **both** ends of its life — twice in a day and twice a month apart are not the same finding. The call site takes `Date.now()` once and hands the same instant to `selectMemories` and to the document, so the two cannot straddle a minute boundary and disagree inside one prompt.

The prompt rule changed with it. `Do not open a reply with a number, a count, or the time` forbade the very citation SOUL.md asks for, so it is gone; in its place she is told to cite a date or a count whenever it earns the line and **never to invent one** — if the record does not say when, she does not know when. SOUL.md is untouched: its own restraint about numbers is scoped to "facts about your situation" (the CONTEXT counts), which is craft about not sounding like a stats readout, and is unrelated to citing her record.

**One accepted consequence: the selection budget now under-counts.** `BUDGET_CHARS` (3000) charges `entry.text.length` only — never the bullet, the `(reached N times)` suffix, the headings, or threads at all — so it was always an approximation. The stamps add roughly 19 characters per entry that it does not model, so a record large enough to hit the cap renders longer than the cap suggests. Left alone deliberately: charging for them would silently make her remember fewer things, which is a bigger change than adding dates and was not what was asked for.

**The launcher swap says what it did (2026-09-01).** The rotation was reported stuck: a coloured face on the home screen, the same one for days. Ruled out with evidence first — `faceFor` gives a different face every day across the cycle, `withRotatingIcons` is registered and generates an alias plus drawables per face, and `requireNativeModule('AppIcon')` throws at import if the module is unlinked, so the app launching at all proves it is there. What could **not** be checked was whether the swap works, because `syncLauncherIcon` caught every failure and returned void: a launcher that refuses it and one that takes it were the same observable event. `getIcon()` had been on the native module since the beginning, built to answer exactly this, and **nothing had ever called it**.

`launcher.ts` records the outcome (`launcherResult()`, session-only, deliberately not a store) and exposes `readLauncherFace()`. The verdict comes from **reading the state back**, not from `setIcon`'s return value — that returns false both for "already showing" and for "no such face", which are not the same thing. A `LAUNCHER` row under PREFERENCES surfaced it: the face the home screen was wearing against the one today asked for, in danger red when they disagreed.

**That row is gone (2026-09-04).** It was a diagnostic, and the diagnosis came back clean — the icon and the splash were confirmed rotating daily on the phone, so the row was reporting agreement every time anyone opened the screen. `launcherResult()` and `readLauncherFace()` stay in `launcher.ts` with their tests: they are what the next investigation would reach for, and `syncLauncherIcon` still reads the state back after every swap. Nothing consumes that read now, which costs one `getIcon()` call per background transition — deliberate, so the verification does not have to be rebuilt to be used again.

**What this still does not tell you**, and the reason it was built before touching the trigger: agreement here means the *aliases* moved. A home screen still showing an older face after that is the launcher's own cache — `ICONS.md` warns some hold the icon until a reboot or redraw — which is a different problem with a different fix. Disagreement points the other way, at the trigger: `syncLauncherIcon` runs only on `AppState → background`, there is no daily job behind it (the WorkManager job `ICONS.md` describes was never built; v2 has no background work at all), and it sits after `void extractNow()` in the same handler, where a synchronous throw would skip it.

**The PROMPT screen is the debugging surface, and is meant to be pasteable whole (2026-08-31).** It carries both calls — her reply and the extraction that follows it — and every field exists because something was once indistinguishable without it. Beyond the request itself: `emotion` is the state **after** the clamp, read against the emotion inside the raw reply (the two differing is the clamp firing, invisible anywhere else); `spoke` is the line the subject actually saw, and under a notice it says the app took the turn and prints the line she never got to show; `took` separates a 25s timeout from a slow provider from an instant refusal; `--- RAW REPLY (N chars, COULD NOT BE READ) ---` puts the verdict in the header and the length beside it, which is the only clue to a reply cut off by the 300-token budget — valid text that stops mid-object. **Text on the file screens is `selectable`**, so a screenshot is not the only way off the phone. **It holds live calls only (2026-09-11).** `speak('open')` fires on every launch and a poked optic fires on every tap, and each of those used to overwrite `lastPrompt` — so the one screen built to read the last request to the model almost always held a greeting that had made no request, printed `(answered from the authored bank. no request was made.)`, and showed **no SOUL.md at all**. The last real prompt was gone before the screen could be reached. `PromptRecord.outcome` narrows to `'model' | 'fallback'`, which makes the invariant the type's business, and the bank branch in `buildReplyMd` is deleted rather than left unreachable. What this gives up is the old argument that recording a bank reply answered "why did that reply look canned" — it never earned displacing the real record, and the canned-looking case that genuinely needs diagnosing is a **fallback**, which is a live call and is still recorded with its failure beside it.

What it still does not hold: **only the last exchange of each kind**, so a failure two messages back is gone, and no provider `finish_reason` (deliberate — surfacing it changes `callLlm`'s return type and every mock of it, and the char count answers the same question).

**An empty 200 is a failure, and extraction has its own budget (2026-08-31).** `callLlm` used to return the empty string when a 200 carried no text, so the caller read it as a model that had answered unreadably — on the PROMPT screen, `RAW REPLY (0 chars)` and nowhere further to go. Two real failures land there: **OpenRouter returns some upstream errors as HTTP 200** with an `error` object and no `choices` (the explaining sentence was being discarded by a reader looking only for text), and a response that spends its whole budget before emitting content. Both now throw, carrying the provider's own sentence where there is one and otherwise the finish reason — `stop_reason` · `finish_reason` · `native_finish_reason` · `finishReason`, so every route is covered. Nothing about `callLlm`'s return type changed; the reason travels in the thrown message into `failure`. And extraction no longer shares the reply's 300-token default: `EXTRACT_MAX_TOKENS` (1000) is passed on the request **and stated in `EXTRACT_SYSTEM` from the same constant**, because a ceiling the model is told about but not given would make it record less for nothing. The two calls were never the same job — a reply is under thirty words, extraction rewrites a file covering the whole pending queue (capped at 40) — and a merely tight ceiling became impossible as that queue grew: every failure leaves more to write up on the next attempt against the same limit, a loop that cannot escape on its own.

**Why a call failed is recorded, and two failures speak in the app's own voice (2026-08-28).** `callLlm` reads the provider's error body and throws it — `http <status>: <the provider's own sentence>` — so `PromptRecord.failure` and `ExtractRecord.failure` carry a reason and the PROMPT screen prints it under `--- NO REPLY ---`. Before this, `raw: null` was the whole story and an exhausted account, a retired model and a dead socket were one indistinguishable line. `SystemCode` is now `'auth' | 'quota'`: a **401/403** is the credential's fault and marks a row under ACCESS, while a **402, or a 400/429 whose body names a credit balance or `insufficient_quota`**, is the account's — the key is valid, so `authFails` is untouched and the wording says to add credit rather than replace the key. A plain 429 rate limit is deliberately *not* quota: it is transient and nothing the subject can fix. Everything else stays hers, covered by an authored line.

Anything under 8 characters, containing whitespace, or matching none of the five is refused at the gate. The accepted consequence: a **valid** key from an unlisted provider (Mistral, Together, xAI, Groq) is refused. Requests go straight from the device to the matched provider — there is no server in this project. A key on the device is readable by anyone holding the phone; it is the subject's own.

## Conventions & gotchas

- All copy verbatim from `T` in `src/shell/strings.ts` (and each module's own `strings.ts`); all hex from `palette` in `src/core/theme.ts`. Don't "improve" either, and don't add strings the design doc doesn't have.
- Verify with `npm test` and `npx tsc --noEmit`. Jest is pinned to 29 (jest-expo 57 requires it); RTL v14 is fully async (`await render(...)`, `await fireEvent.press(...)`, **`await view.unmount()`** — omitting that last one causes overlapping `act()` and `toJSON()` returning null); jest uses the `react-native-worklets/jest/resolver.js` resolver.
- **No test may make a real network request.** Mock `src/vera/llm.ts` with a `jest.requireActual` spread so `AuthError` stays a real class for `speech.ts`'s `instanceof`, and mock `expo-secure-store` with an in-memory vault (the OS keystore has no jsdom implementation).
- Skia can't run in jest (jsdom); it's mocked as a no-op Proxy in `jest.setup.ts`. **Never put logic inside a Skia component** — it is invisible to tests. Skia now carries two things: the optic (`Optic.tsx`, continuous animation) and Specimen-03's blob (`modules/organism/Blob.tsx`).
- **Two builds, side by side (2026-08-28).** `app.config.js` overlays `app.json` and reads `EAS_BUILD_PROFILE`: the **development** profile builds `com.youngmookk.thefacility.dev` named "V.E.R.A. dev", everything else builds the shipping id. Both install at once, with separate data. Unset — a local prebuild, `expo run:android`, Metro — defaults to the **shipping** identity on purpose: a local build landing on the installed app is an annoyance, a cloud build going out under `.dev` is a wrong package id no later build can take back. `--profile development` needs `npx expo start` running to load any JS at all; `--profile preview` bundles the JS in and runs anywhere, at the price of a rebuild for **every** change. Either way, a native dependency, an `app.json` change or an SDK bump needs a rebuild.
- **`expo-secure-store` is native and the two new fonts are new assets, so a dev build installed before 2026-07-30 cannot load v2.** The one on the phone is current. Build another with `npx eas-cli@latest build -p android --profile development`, and expect to need one whenever a native dependency moves. **The deferred patch bumps were taken on 2026-08-19** (expo 57.0.14, react-native 0.86.2, reanimated 4.5.1, worklets 0.10.1, expo-dev-client 57.0.13, expo-splash-screen 57.0.7, jest-expo 57.0.4), so **the dev build on the phone predates them and needs replacing** — five of the seven are native. `npx expo install --fix` **cannot do this update**: it installs in two passes, and the second fails ERESOLVE because `react-native@0.86.2` peer-wants `@react-native/jest-preset@0.86.2` while `jest-expo` — which is what actually governs that peer, and which the fixer leaves at its old range in `package.json` — still pins 0.86.0. Do not take npm's suggestion of `--force`/`--legacy-peer-deps` (see `.npmrc` below). The fix is to let `--fix` write `package.json`, then `rm -rf node_modules package-lock.json && npm install` for a single clean resolve. Profiles live in `eas.json` (Android APK, `internal` distribution, node pinned 24.14.1). Run with `npx expo start` (auto-detects `expo-dev-client`).
- **After a React Native version bump, build with `--clear-cache` or the APK will crash on launch.** The 2026-08-19 build was made from the correct commit with a correct lockfile (RN 0.86.2) and still died two seconds in, silently, with nothing in the Metro terminal. `adb logcat -b crash -d` gave the cause: `libworklets.so` aborting on `jsi::Value::getString(): assertion "isString()" failed`, with the header path reading **`react-android-0.86.0`**. EAS's Gradle cache had compiled worklets against the *old* react-android prefab while `npm ci` installed the new React Native — an ABI mismatch, so worklets read a `jsi::Value` with the wrong memory layout. `npx eas-cli build -p android --profile development --clear-cache` fixed it. **`npm test` and `tsc` cannot see this class of failure at all** — Skia and reanimated are jest no-ops, so nothing in the suite touches worklets' native side. A real build on a real device is the only detector.
- **`expo-haptics` was added on 2026-09-04 and is native, so the dev build on the phone cannot feel it** until the next `--profile development` build. `npx expo install expo-haptics` resolved it cleanly — one package, `~57.0.2`, no other line in the lockfile moved. It is mocked globally in `jest.setup.ts` rather than per-file, because every test that renders the Stage imports it transitively and a buzz is the one piece of feedback a test can never observe on its own.

- **`expo-notifications` is back, for two rooms now (2026-09-04, second one 2026-09-07)**, and it is native — the dev build on the phone cannot show a card until the next `--profile development` build. `@react-native-community/slider` is still gone. Two of the three clauses of the v2 decision fall: there are notifications, and there is a permission ceremony. **The third holds — there is still no background work of any kind** — and `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-04-daily-advice-design.md` §3 is the consequence of holding it: a repeating trigger cannot vary its content, and nothing of ours is awake at 09:00, so ninety cards are handed to Android with their text already written into them and the queue runs dry after ninety days of not opening the app.
- **`.npmrc` (committed) pins `legacy-peer-deps=false`** — a global `~/.npmrc` with legacy-peer-deps=true silently drops `@react-native/babel-preset` + its metro/hermes peer subtree from the lockfile, which breaks EAS Build's strict `npm ci`. Keep it; don't regenerate the lock with legacy-peer-deps on.
- No navigation library (conditional render on `view` **is** the router), no raster UI assets, fonts bundled locally (no CDN).
- Repo style is single quotes. An editor formatter here has previously reformatted files to double quotes — don't let it.
